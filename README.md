# Sundance

An isometric, pixel-art spaghetti western in Godot 4.7, drawing on Leone, Corbucci and
Sollima: a stealth shooter about a gun for hire. One stranger, five towns, whoever pays.
Every sprite and sound is generated by the Python tools in `tools/`; nothing is drawn
by hand.

| Town | What it asks of him |
|---|---|
| San Miguel | The mayor pays to be rid of a gang, who hang a villager at noon. |
| Cruz Verde | His partner is held in a revolutionary fort, the morning after a victory party. |
| Paso Esperanza | The revolution pays him to cover a wrecked train's unloading against the army. |
| Silencio | Two gangs snowed in under a truce, and stolen gold to carry off. |
| A Good Man Never Dies | His own funeral: everyone he rode for or against has come to see him buried. |

[DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) says how the game works, system by system.
[CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) holds the working notes: conventions, testing and the asset commands.

## Play

The baked assets are committed, so the game runs straight from a clone. Open the folder in
Godot 4.7 and press Play, or run:

```bash
godot --path .
```

The title card leads to the town picker; a town opens on its briefing (who's paying, the
job, who's who), then the loadout, then the town. The job is repeated in the Esc menu,
arrows point to it as you ride in (and while Tab is held), and the lines under the enemy
count say where it stands.

| Keys | |
|---|---|
| WASD, mouse | move, aim |
| LMB, RMB | fire; fan the hammer, or fire both barrels |
| C or Ctrl | crouch |
| R | reload (fire stops a partial reload) |
| 1 2 3, wheel | switch guns |
| F | take the gun at your feet |
| E | knife; hold to drag a body. Also cuts a man loose, works the machine gun, the plunger and the strongbox, and hammers at a coffin lid |
| B, G, Q | bandage, throw dynamite, toss a peso |
| Tab (hold) | arrows to what the town asks of you |
| Esc | back a step; in town, the menu (paused): resume, the keys, start over, another town, leave |
| Enter | after a win, on to the next town; after a loss, the same town again |

The Esc menu's THE KEYS shows the same list in the game, with what E does in that town.

On the loadout screen, 1/2/3 or a click go through a slot's guns (those you can afford), B
and G buy a bandage and a stick of dynamite, and Enter rides in. Guns and kit cost points
out of the town's budget: Silencio 8, San Miguel 12, Cruz Verde 14, Paso Esperanza 18. A
Good Man Never Dies skips it: he has his pair of Colts and nothing spare.

The window can be any size. A bigger one shows more of the town, up to about 640x360 world
pixels on a 16:9 screen (wider on a 21:9 one); past that the pixels only get bigger.

Two things are kept between sittings, in Godot's user folder: his record from each town won,
which the funeral's sermon reads out (`user://record.json`), and the first-time hints
already shown (`user://hints.json`). The picker's last row forgets either.

## Where things are

- `scripts/`: the game. `game.gd` builds a town and runs its rules; `director.gd` brings
  waves and help in; `player.gd`, `enemy.gd` and `civilian.gd` are the people (sharing
  `actor.gd`); `hud.gd` draws the HUD and every menu screen. Each objective has its own
  script: `hanging.gd`, `captive.gd`, `cargo.gd`, `strongbox.gd`, `coffin.gd` and
  `funeral.gd`. Gun behaviour is data, in `Weapon.DATA` (`weapon.gd`).
- `data/`: `levels.json` (the towns, their briefings, budgets and waves), `enemies.json`
  (bandit and townsfolk types, loadouts, palettes), `hints.json` (the death tips, the
  first-time hints and the keys page), and one map per town, written by its
  `tools/make_*.py`.
- `tools/`: the asset generator and the map scripts (below), with `anim_sheet.py` (a contact
  sheet of baked frames) and `map_plan.py` (a map's layout on its tile grid).
- `tests/`: headless test scripts, one per feature (below).
- `assets/`: generated sprites, sounds and `meta.json`; `shaders/`: the palette swap, the
  post-process grade, silhouettes and snowfall.

## Assets

Every sprite and sound is generated by `tools/gen_assets.py`: a small point-splat 3D renderer
draws the 8-direction characters, buildings and props in 2:1 isometric pixel art, and the
sounds are synthesised. Each map is laid out by its own script, `tools/make_town.py`
(San Miguel, `data/town.json`), `tools/make_cruz_verde.py` (`data/cruz_verde.json`),
`tools/make_train.py` (Paso Esperanza, `data/train.json`), `tools/make_silencio.py`
(`data/silencio.json`) and `tools/make_funeral.py` (A Good Man Never Dies,
`data/funeral.json`), sharing `tools/layout.py`, which refuses overlapping objects or anyone
placed inside things and writes the guard posts. A map with a `"ground"` key gets its own
ground baked by `gen_assets.py ground` (`"snow"` bakes it white, with `"ice"` for frozen
water; `"weather": "grey"` bakes wet grey earth). Objects in `world.SNOWY` are baked a second
time under snow, their `"snow"` skin, which a map with `"skin": "snow"` (Silencio) wears in
place of the plain look; they're lit under cloud (`render(overcast=True)`, faint shadows) to
go with its `"weather": "snow"`. Character animations are baked in parallel from key poses
in `tools/chars.py` and packed into one atlas per character; baking every body takes ten
minutes or more, so re-bake only the ones you changed. The `music` step writes each level's
score (`tools/music.py`, five minutes or so) to `assets/music/`; `tools/music.py silencio`
writes the layers and a mix of all three to `build/music/` to listen to.

```bash
python3 -m venv tools/.venv && tools/.venv/bin/pip install -r tools/requirements.txt
tools/.venv/bin/python tools/gen_assets.py            # everything
tools/.venv/bin/python tools/gen_assets.py chars ui   # or just some: chars world fx ui sfx animals ground music
SUNDANCE_TEMPLATES=veteran tools/.venv/bin/python tools/gen_assets.py chars   # re-bake some bodies
SUNDANCE_OBJECTS=coop,hencrate tools/.venv/bin/python tools/gen_assets.py world   # re-bake some world objects
SUNDANCE_GROUNDS=train tools/.venv/bin/python tools/gen_assets.py ground   # re-bake some maps' grounds (town, cruz_verde, train...)
godot --headless --path . --import
```

## Tests and debug flags

`tools/run_tests.sh` runs them all, eight at a time, longest first (by last run's times,
kept in `.godot/test_times`), and lists what failed (logs in `.godot/test_logs/`);
`tools/run_tests.sh tests/test_kegs.gd` runs some of them. Each is an `extends SceneTree`
script that stages one feature and prints `FAIL: ...` lines, ending `... test: OK`.
test_ride also holds each town's sprites to a video memory budget (`BUDGET_MB`). In a
window, every town that comes up writes a `Town up:` line to the log (the graphics card,
the video memory held, the build time): on Windows `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Sundance\logs\`,
on macOS `~/Library/Application Support/Godot/app_userdata/Sundance/logs/`. Or run

Flags go after `--`: `--skip-title` (no title, briefing or loadout screen; rides in with Colt, coach gun and Winchester, or `--guns=navy,spencer`, one a slot), `--level=ID` (a level from `data/levels.json`, e.g. `san_miguel_35`), `--at=GX,GY` (start tile), `--bot` (autopilot that shoots
and logs hits and bandit reactions; `--bot=miss` shoots just past them), `--alert` (trigger the close-up), `--rattle` (near-miss every bandit once), `--gasp` (every downed man tries his last gasp), `--throw-at=GX,GY` and `--toss-at=GX,GY` (where a stick or a peso lands, in place of the mouse), `--freeze` (no title, everyone stands still), `--nails=N` (nails in his coffin lid at the funeral before the sermon adds any; `--at` starts him out of it), `--record=good|middling|bad|worst` (a made-up record for the funeral's sermon, in place of the one his wins have kept), `--shot=PATH --shot-time=SECONDS` (save a
screenshot and quit). The window opens maximized; a screenshot at a set size needs a small
script that calls `DisplayServer.window_set_mode(WINDOW_MODE_WINDOWED)` and
`window_set_size` before loading the scene (headless runs always play at 1280x720).

## Releases

Publishing a release on GitHub (Releases > Draft a new release, a tag like `v0.1.0`,
Publish) runs `.github/workflows/release.yml`. It exports the three presets in
`export_presets.cfg` with Godot 4.7.2 on a GitHub Linux runner and attaches them to the
release some minutes later:

- `sundance-0.1.0-windows.zip`: one `Sundance.exe`, the game packed inside
- `sundance-0.1.0-macos.zip`: `Sundance.app`, universal (Intel and Apple Silicon)
- `sundance-0.1.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz`: one `Sundance.x86_64`
- `SHA256SUMS`

The tag, less its `v`, is written into `application/config/version` for the build and shown
in the corner of the title card; a build from the editor has no version and shows none.
Actions > Release > Run workflow makes the same builds as a workflow artifact, without a
release, to try the pipeline out. No tests run in it.

The builds aren't signed (the Mac app only ad hoc), so worth a line in the release notes:
on Windows, SmartScreen's "More info > Run anyway"; on macOS, after unzipping,
`xattr -cr Sundance.app` (or System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway).

To export locally, install the 4.7.2 export templates (Editor > Manage Export Templates),
then `godot --headless --path . --export-release macOS build/macos/Sundance.zip`
(or `Windows`, `Linux`); `build/` is ignored.
